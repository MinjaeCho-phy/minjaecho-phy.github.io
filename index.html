01 / Research

How can we test
stringy gravity?

My work connects the symmetry and matter couplings of Double Field Theory to predictions we can confront with observations.

01

Gravity & cosmic evolution

Double Field Theory cosmology

I study cosmological dynamics in a framework that brings the metric, the Kalb–Ramond field, and the dilaton into a common gravitational sector. My interests include matter–dilaton couplings, cosmic expansion, and the conditions under which general-relativistic behavior and a frozen dilaton emerge.

O(D,D) symmetry · Dilaton dynamics · Late-time attractors

02

Fundamental constants

A cosmic history of the fine-structure constant

The evolution of the dilaton can leave a signature in the fine-structure constant, α. I investigate how atomic clocks, quasar absorption lines, JWST spectra, and CMB constraints can probe this history—and distinguish models with similar cosmic expansion.

Varying α · Atomic clocks · Cosmological spectroscopy

03

Data & inference

From cosmological models to observational constraints

I combine supernovae, cosmic chronometers, and measurements of fundamental constants to estimate model parameters and compare predictions with ΛCDM. I pay particular attention to parameter degeneracies, prior sensitivity, Bayesian evidence, and observational systematics.

Bayesian inference · MCMC & nested sampling · Model comparison

↗

A developing direction

Matter couplings & precision tests

I am also exploring how microscopic dilaton couplings translate into composite-body masses and scalar charges, and how cosmological models connect to equivalence-principle and Solar System tests.

02 / Approach

From a symmetry
to a testable prediction.

I work across theoretical analysis, numerical computation, and statistical inference.

I

Formulate

Translate the structure of DFT and its matter couplings into cosmological equations and observable quantities.

II

Compute

Implement models in Python, solve their dynamics, and build analysis pipelines using SimpleMC.

III

Constrain

Use posterior distributions and model evidence to assess what the data support, and how conclusions depend on assumptions.
